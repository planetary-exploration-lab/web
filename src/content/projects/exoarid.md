Our project integrated a natural experiment (aridity transects in the Atacama Desert, Chile) and lab-based characterization to investigate the mechanisms through which organisms adapt to aridity (see figure). It also sought to disentangle the noted association of xerotolerance with ionizing radiation (IR) resistance. The project built on prior systematic sampling along variable aridity transects in the Atacama, including extensive prior characterization of soil parameters, ribosomal (16S) community characterization, and cultivation work. Its aims were to (**1a**) provide a comprehensive view of microbial community structure, function, and activity along these transects; (**1b**) confirm aridity-associated responses and in situ activity through a focused field study; (**2a**) refine mechanisms of xerotolerance using isolate studies; and (**2b**) disentangle xerotolerance and IR resistance, integrating genomic, transcriptomic, physical, chemical, and physiological responses.
